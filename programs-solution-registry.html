Vetted AI solutions, in plain terms

The AI marketplace is crowded, fast-moving, and full of claims that are hard to check.

A superintendent choosing a classroom AI tool and a plant manager choosing a process-optimisation platform are both being asked to evaluate technology that did not exist two years ago, against vendors with far more information than they have.

The Hub does that evaluation instead. We assess AI products against published criteria — effectiveness, data security, responsible use, and fit with Rhode Island’s regulatory and operational requirements — and publish the results in the AI RI Hub Solution Registry.

What a listing means. We do not sell software, we take no vendor fees, and a listing is a starting point for your own diligence, not a substitute for it. Read our evaluation criteria and conflict-of-interest policy.

Sectors we cover

The Registry covers all six sectors identified by the Governor’s AI Task Force.

Education

Classroom platforms, evaluated against Rhode Island’s student data privacy and instructional effectiveness standards.

Health

Clinical decision support, administrative applications, and healthcare data ethics.

Finance

AI risk management, compliance, and algorithmic transparency.

Small business, startups & nonprofits

Accessible tools for organisations without technical staff.

Government

Deployment, procurement guidance, and responsible-AI policy fit.

Defense industries & maritime technologies

Production optimisation, predictive maintenance, and cybersecurity for connected systems.

Manufacturing

Real-time AI platforms now integrate directly with existing industrial control systems to monitor operations, optimise performance, reduce energy use, and increase uptime. We look for sector-specific expertise, integration with standard industrial protocols, strong data security, and a demonstrated operational track record — so a Rhode Island manufacturer can start without hiring a data scientist first.

K-12 education

We evaluate classroom AI platforms against Rhode Island’s standards for student data privacy, instructional effectiveness, and responsible use. That means FERPA and COPPA compliance, independent security certification, a commitment not to use student or teacher data to train models, and integration with the learning management systems districts already run. We work directly with the Rhode Island Department of Education to align our criteria with state policy, and we give preference to platforms that help students understand and question the AI they are using.

How vetting works

  1. A vendor applies, or a Rhode Island organisation nominates a product it is considering.
  2. The Hub evaluates it against the published criteria for that sector, with technical input from partners at URI and the Institute at RIC.
  3. Products that meet the criteria are listed in the Registry with a plain-language summary of what they do, what they cost, and what the evaluation found.
  4. Listings are reviewed on a fixed cycle. AI products change fast; a listing is not permanent.
